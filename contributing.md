# Contributing

Know a useful tool or spotted something out of date? [Open an issue](https://github.com/ilyaspiridonov/awesome-sales-tools/issues/new/choose) or send a pull request. 

## What's a good fit

Recommend tools you've used or evaluated that are available, maintained, and have public documentation. Explain what they help someone do and why you'd choose them over an option already listed - for exampe: a different workflow, audience, integration, or self-hosting option etc.

General-purpose tools need a clear sales or GTM use. Related Resources is for directories and review sites that help readers compare B2B sales software or learn more.

## Out of scope

- Agencies, consulting services, waitlists, discontinued products and abandoned projects.
- Duplicate listings, affiliate links and tracking links.
- Products mainly designed for fake accounts or engagement, impersonation, untargeted promotional posting or messaging or evading moderation or access restrictions.

## Suggesting or adding a tool

Include its URL, what it helps you do, why you recommend it. When opening an issue or creating a PR, provide links to evidence or supporting documentation. Tell us if you're connected to the product.

For a pull request:

- Add one new product at a time, alphabetically under its main use. List it once, even if it does several jobs.
- Use `- [Name](https://url) - Product description. What you can do with it.` Link to the official website or repository. A source link can also go in the PR description.
- Write two short sentences: what it is, then the work it helps someone do. Start the second with an action verb. Related resources can use one sentence.
- Write naturally, for example: "Run email follow-ups". 
- Give readers a concrete reason to consider it. Avoid invented differences or a second sentence that just repeats the category. 
- Skip slogans, superlatives, and price amounts. Use normal capitalization and end sentences with periods.

Example: `Digital sales rooms with document tracking. Share content, track engagement, and coordinate a mutual action plan.`

GitHub runs the checks. Optional local check: `npx --yes awesome-lint@2.3.0`.

## Changing sections or jobs

Propose changes in an issue or pull request. Explain what readers need to find, why the current structure is not ideal, and which entries would move.

For a new job, describe the outcome, how it differs from jobs already covered, and a tool that supports it.

For new sections or splits, show a useful distinction and at least three qualifying tools per section. For renames or merges, explain how browsing improves. A new data source or an AI label alone doesn't need its own section.

Update the contents, affected entries, and links to renamed sections together. Taxonomy and maintenance changes can cover multiple entries. The [RevManic taxonomy](https://revmanic.com/jobs) is a reference.

## Editorial independence

Founders and vendors are welcome. Disclose your connection; descriptions may be edited for clarity and consistency. Payment, backlink exchanges, or requests for stars never determine inclusion or ordering.

## Keeping the list current

Review a few sections at a time: check links, names, capabilities, and important limits. Include sources with corrections. Review acquisitions and renames; remove discontinued tools and entries that no longer qualify.
